📈 Golden Cross Rotation Strategy

How the TradeCraft Alpaca runner picks stocks, enters, exits, and rotates positions — explained end to end.

🎯 Strategy Philosophy

This is a trend-following, momentum-based rotation strategy designed for the S&P 1500 universe. The core idea: buy stocks that are just beginning a strong upward trend, hold them while the trend persists, and replace them when better opportunities emerge or the trend reverses.

It's called a "rotation" strategy because the portfolio constantly turns over — old positions get sold (rotated out) and new ones get bought (rotated in) as the market's strongest signals shift from one stock to another. The portfolio always holds the top 5 ranked stocks from the daily scan.

Key insight: The strategy doesn't try to predict the market. It reacts to what the price action is already telling us — EMAs crossing signal a trend change, RSI confirms momentum, and volume confirms conviction. It's systematic, rules-based, and removes emotional decision-making.

🚦 Entry Signals

Every day, the strategy scans all ~1,500 S&P 1500 stocks looking for two types of entry signals. A stock only needs one to be considered.

Entry A — Primary

Golden Cross EMA20 × EMA200

The 20-day exponential moving average crosses above the 200-day moving average within the last 5 trading days. This is a classic long-term bullish signal — the short-term trend has decisively turned upward relative to the long-term trend.

EMA20 crosses above EMA200 in last 5 days

The crossover angle is measured: how fast are the two EMAs diverging? A steeper angle = stronger trend = higher score.

Entry B — Secondary

Momentum Continuation Stacked EMAs + RSI

For stocks that are already in an uptrend but haven't had a recent golden cross, this signal catches strong momentum continuations. Three conditions must all be true:

  • Price > EMA50 > EMA200 — bullish stack (uptrend confirmed at multiple timeframes)
  • RSI(14) > 60 — momentum is present (not overbought, but strong)
  • Volume > 1.2× 50-day average — institutional conviction behind the move
P > EMA50 > EMA200 && RSI > 60 && Vol > 1.2× VolMA50

📊 Ranking & Scoring

Every stock that triggers an entry signal gets a composite score from 0 to 1. The top 5 scorers become the day's candidates.

Score = 0.60 × Anglenorm + 0.40 × Capnorm

Why 60/40 split? The angle measures technical strength (is the trend real and accelerating?), while market cap measures fundamental safety (is this a liquid, established company?). The 60/40 weighting prioritizes technical signal while still favoring larger caps.

🚪 Exit Signals

Once a stock is in the portfolio, it stays until any one of these conditions is met:

Exit

Death Cross

The 20-day EMA crosses below the 200-day EMA. This is the inverse of the golden cross — the short-term trend has turned bearish relative to the long-term trend. Immediate sell, no hold period.

Exit

Rotation Out

The stock falls out of the top 5 ranked candidates. A newer, stronger signal has replaced it. But: the stock must have been held for at least 10 trading days before it can be rotated out — this prevents churning good positions on daily noise.

Risk

Trailing Stop (20%)

A 20% trailing stop is attached to every position from day one (GTC order). If the stock drops 20% from its peak since entry, the stop triggers and the position is sold. This is your catastrophic loss protection — no single position can lose more than ~20%.

Risk

Take Profit (20%)

A 20% take-profit limit order is attached at entry. If the stock rises 20% above the entry price, the position is sold for a profit. This locks in gains and frees up capital for the next opportunity.

Risk — Market-Level

Crisis Override

If the SPY ETF drops more than 20% from its 200-day high, the strategy assumes a market crash. All positions are closed to cash immediately. The strategy stays in cash until SPY recovers. This is a circuit breaker, not a timing signal.

🔄 The Daily Pipeline

Here's exactly what happens every time the strategy runs (scheduled weekdays at 8 PM ET):

📡 Fetch latest date → 🛡️ Check crisis override → 🔍 Scan 1,500 stocks → 🏆 Rank top 5 → 🔒 Re-attach stops → ❌ Close stale positions → ✅ Open new positions

Step-by-step breakdown

  1. Fetch the latest trading date from the PostgreSQL database (SELECT MAX("Date") FROM aapl). This is the "as of" date for all analysis.
  2. Check crisis override — query SPY's 200-day high. If drawdown > 20%, close everything and go to cash. Otherwise proceed.
  3. Scan all 1,500 stocks — for each ticker, pull the last 300 days of OHLCV data from the database. Compute EMAs (20, 50, 200), RSI(14), and volume MA(50). Check for Entry A (golden cross in last 5 days) and Entry B (momentum continuation).
  4. Rank candidates — every stock with a valid entry signal gets scored using the 60/40 angle + market cap formula. Sort descending, take the top 5.
  5. Re-attach trailing stops — for every existing position, check if a trailing stop order is active. If missing (e.g., from a previous "day" TIF order that expired), submit a new GTC trailing stop at 20%.
  6. Close stale positions — for each current position:
    • If it has a death cross → sell immediately
    • If it's not in the top 5 and held ≥ 10 days → sell (rotated out)
    • If it's not in the top 5 but held < 10 days → hold (give it time)
  7. Open new positions — count how many slots are available (MAX_HOLDINGS = 5 minus remaining positions). For each available slot, take the next candidate from the ranked list and submit a GTC bracket order (market entry + 20% take-profit + 20% trailing stop).

🔄 How Rotation Works — Example

Day 1: The scan finds AXP, GS, JPM, SCHW, WFC as the top 5. The strategy buys all 5 with bracket orders.

Day 2–10: The strategy runs daily. All 5 positions are still in the top 5, so nothing happens. Trailing stops are re-attached if needed.

Day 11: A new stock (MSFT) has a golden cross and scores higher than WFC. The new top 5 is: AXP, GS, JPM, MSFT, SCHW.

  • WFC is no longer in the top 5 → rotated out (held 11 days ≥ 10, so it's sold)
  • MSFT is new → rotated in with a fresh bracket order
  • AXP, GS, JPM, SCHW are still in the top 5 → held

Day 15: JPM gets a death cross (EMA20 crosses below EMA200). Even though it's still in the top 5, the death cross overrides — sold immediately. The next candidate fills the slot.

Day 20: AXP hits the 20% take-profit. The bracket's limit order fills automatically. The next candidate fills the slot.

⚙️ Technical Implementation

Data Source

All price data comes from a PostgreSQL database (sp1500_1d) containing daily OHLCV for all S&P 1500 stocks. Each ticker has its own table (e.g., aapl, msft). The database is updated daily via a separate ETL process.

Execution

Orders are submitted to Alpaca Markets (paper trading) via their REST API. The strategy uses:

  • Bracket orders for entries — market order to buy, with attached OCO take-profit + trailing-stop
  • Market orders for exits — sell at market when a close signal triggers
  • Standalone trailing stops for re-attaching protection to existing positions
  • All protection orders use GTC (good-till-canceled) time-in-force so they persist beyond the entry day

Scheduling

The strategy runs via cron at 8 PM ET on weekdays. A heartbeat file tracks the last run's success/failure status for monitoring.

💡 Summary

In one sentence: Every day, scan 1,500 stocks for those just beginning a strong uptrend, buy the 5 best ones with built-in profit-taking and stop-losses, and replace them as better opportunities emerge or trends reverse.

Strengths: Systematic, rules-based, removes emotion, multiple layers of risk management, daily rebalancing keeps the portfolio fresh.
Weaknesses: Trend-following strategies struggle in choppy/sideways markets, can whipsaw during quick reversals, relies on daily data (misses intraday moves).
